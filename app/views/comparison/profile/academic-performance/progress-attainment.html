Progress 8

2024 to 2025 academic year

Progress 8 is a performance measure used to show how much progress pupils make from the end of primary school (KS2) to the end of secondary school (KS4) across their best performing 8 subjects.

Find out more about Progress 8 scores and confidence intervals

Progress 8 shows how much progress a pupil made compared to other pupils with similar starting points at the end of primary school.

The score shows progress compared to the national average:

  • a score of 0 means students made average progress compared to others with similar starting points
  • a positive score of more than 0 means pupils made more progress than average
  • a negative score of less than 0 means students made less progress than average

Progress 8 scores are influenced by both the school and their pupils. It is difficult to determine how much of the progress score is due to the school and how much is due to the pupils themselves. Confidence intervals reflect this uncertainty.

If the confidence intervals for two schools overlap, it means we cannot be sure that one school’s Progress 8 score is better than the other.

The greater the number of pupils, the smaller the range of the confidence interval. Smaller schools have larger confidence intervals because fewer pupils are included, so the score is more affected by the performance of individual pupils.  

  • A school is above average if its Progress 8 score is above 0 and the entire confidence interval is above 0
  • A school is below average if its Progress 8 score is below 0 and the entire confidence interval is below 0.
data_info_alert

There are no Progress 8 scores for the academic years 2024 to 2025 and 2025 to 2026, as there is no KS2 assessment data available to calculate the baseline. This is due to COVID-19 disruption.

What you could ask schools about pupil progress

When researching, contacting or visiting a school, you may want to ask:

  • what are the differences in progress between different groups of pupils, for example disadvantaged pupils or pupils with SEND?
  • what support is available to each year group?
  • how does the school monitor pupil progress?
  • how does the school report pupil progress to parents and carers?

Attainment 8

2024 to 2025 academic year

Attainment 8 is used to assess a pupil’s average achievement across their 8 best GCSE-level subjects.

Find out more about Attainment 8

The 8 subjects that are used in Attainment 8 are split into 3 groups:

  • English and maths, which are both double-weighted
  • EBacc subjects, these are a group of GCSE subjects that help students gain a broad set of knowledge and skills. These subjects include sciences, geography, history, and languages
  • open group which includes any other approved GCSEs or vocational qualifications

Each GCSE grade is given a point score (for example grade 9 = 9 points, grade 8 = 8 points, and so on). The scores from the best 8 subjects (with English and Maths counting twice) are added together to give the Attainment 8 score.

School Score Meaning

Oak Nest Secondary School 48.3 Pupils generally scored the equivalent of between grade 4 and 5 in their 8 best GCSE-level subjects.
Thomas Lane Secondary School 52.3 Pupils generally scored the equivalent of between grade 5 and 6 in their 8 best GCSE-level subjects.
Sheffield average 43.2 Pupils generally scored the equivalent of between grade 4 and 5 in their 8 best GCSE-level subjects.
