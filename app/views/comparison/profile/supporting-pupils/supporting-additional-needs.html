Support for pupils with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND)

All schools in England must support children with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND).

What is SEN and SEND?

SEND stands for special educational needs and disabilities.

Special educational needs (SEN) are learning difficulties or disabilities that mean a pupil needs extra or different support from pupils the same age.

SEN are grouped into four broad areas:

  • communication and interaction – difficulties with speaking, understanding others or social communication
  • cognition and learning – learning at a slower pace than pupils of a similar age
  • social, emotional and mental health – including anxiety, depression or attention difficulties
  • sensory and/or physical needs – disabilities that make it harder to access learning or school facilities

Children will receive support at school without an education, health and care (EHC) plan. This support includes identifying needs early and making reasonable adjustments.
Find out more about EHC plans.

Specialist provision offered

In special educational needs (SEN) units pupils are taught within separate classes for at least half of their time. In resourced provisions pupils are taught within mainstream classes for at least half of their time, but have access to a base and specialist facilities around the school.

What is a SEN unit or a resourced provision?

SEN (special educational needs) units and resourced provisions are specialist provisions in mainstream schools formally recognised by the local authority.

In SEN units pupils are taught within separate classes for at least half of their time.

In resourced provisions pupils are taught within mainstream classes for at least half of their time, but have access to a base and specialist facilities around the school.

They are both for pupils with SEN, who typically have education, health, and care (EHC) plans.

School SEND documents

Schools publish documents that you can read to compare their approach to pupils with SEND.

Schools must publish a SEND information report and a SEND policy on their website.

Find out more about SEND information reports

This will outline how a school identifies, supports and monitors pupils with SEND.

A SEND information report includes:

  • the types of SEND provided for
  • how the school identifies SEND
  • how parents are involved in SEND reviews
  • how the school supports pupils emotional and social development

Find out more about SEND policies

A SEND policy explains the school's overall approach, values and responsibilities for supporting pupils with SEND.

A school's SEND policy may include:

  • the school's vision and aim for SEND provision
  • who is responsible for SEND provision
  • what training and development staff have to support pupils with SEND

What you could ask schools about SEND support

When researching, contacting or visiting a school, you may want to ask:

  • what level of support is available for pupils with SEND?
  • what facilities does the school have to support pupils with SEND?
  • how accessible is the school site?
  • what classroom support is available for pupils with SEND?
  • what the school vision for SEND is and when they hope to achieve it?
  • how is the behaviour policy adapted for pupils with SEND or additional needs?
